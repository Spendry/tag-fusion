A practical field guide

How can you discover useful websites beyond a search box?

Updated November 23, 2026.

TagFusion helps you find independent websites through broad topics and smaller labels. Pick a category, then choose tags that describe what you want. Each added tag narrows the list. Open a result and decide for yourself whether it is useful.

Search works well when you already know what to ask for. Browsing can help when you do not. Tags can connect topics that are often kept apart, such as maps and history or poetry and sound. Treat each result as a lead to check, not a recommendation.

How do categories and tags work together?

Categories show the broad kind of website. Tags name a subject or quality. A listing stays in the results only when it has every tag you chose.

  • Creative tools. Places to make, remix, or publish something.
  • Learn & think. Explanations, references, research, and ideas.
  • Independent web. Small, personal, and community-made corners.
  • Play & wander. Games, experiments, and delightful detours.

What does a listing tell you?

That a person looked at the site, found it worth a visit, and described it. It does not check the site’s safety, accuracy, or privacy, and a site can change after it is listed.

How can you assess an unfamiliar website?

Before relying on a new site, ask a few simple questions:

  • Does it say who made it and what it is for?
  • Do important claims link to sources you can check?
  • Can you read its privacy rules before sharing personal details?
  • Can you tell ads apart from independent recommendations?
  • Does the page still work, and does it match the directory description?

A Stanford study of how people judge websites found that readers look for who is behind a site and whether its claims are sourced (Fogg and colleagues, 2001). For health, money, legal, or safety decisions, check an official source or ask a qualified professional. TagFusion is a starting point, not professional advice.

What do visitors often ask?

How do I search the directory?
Open Search to look through the listings by name, description, category, or tag.
How do tags work?
Choose one or more tags. A result must have every tag you chose, so each new tag makes the list smaller.
Does a listing mean TagFusion recommends a site?
No. A listing is a place to start exploring. Check the destination yourself, especially before sharing personal details or relying on important information.
How can I suggest a website?
Use Suggest a website and give its name, address, a short description, a category, and up to four tags. A person reads each suggestion before anything is listed.
Do I need an account?
No. TagFusion has no accounts. Suggesting a site goes through GitHub, which has its own.
